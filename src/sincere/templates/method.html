What the numbers mean

A review's suspicion is the chance at least one of its tells is a true signal (they combine as noisy-OR), discounted by the traits that cost real effort to fake. The score ranks; it never convicts on its own.

What gets taken out is only what the place's own reviewers cannot explain: the five-stars from thin accounts beyond the rate accounts with 4 to 50 reviews give, the reviews in a burst beyond that week's expected count, the pairs posted minutes apart beyond what the hours people post here predict, and every member of an echo or a ring. Bursts are judged against the sample's median week (Poisson tail below 0.001, at least 2.5× the median).

Where this is blind

  • A place where everyone gives five stars looks the same whether they mean it or not. The gradient is the evidence; a flat 95% is not.
  • A first review at a famous place is normal. That is why a thin account alone never counts, only the excess does.
  • Two accounts that share other places are usually a couple. Rings need three.
  • "Newest first" still surfaces old reviews edited recently; they are shown but kept off the axis.
